The archive's reading. The archaeology reads the layer from its output: a book counts as in the model when the model can complete it, and memorization follows 'the frequency with which passages of those books appear on the web'. The archive reads this as bearing on #186's persistence criterion, placement 'where the training and retrieval layers actually look'; the name under which a book is carried stays outside the saved abstract.

What its record can settle

  • whether a book is likely known to ChatGPT or GPT-4, by name cloze membership inference
  • whether a book's presence on the web predicts its memorization
  • whether memorization inflates downstream performance on a book

What it cannot settle

  • which corpus or dataset carried a book into the model
  • whether the model attributes a known book to its author
  • the training data of the models (the paper argues for open models whose training data is known)

Kept apart

  • NOT a list of training data: the books are inferred from the models' behaviour
  • NOT a test of author attribution: the 'name cloze' is its query method, and the saved abstract does not say whose names it masks
  • NOT a corpus: it infers what two closed models have read

Relations

  • neighbour: Min-K% Prob (both test from outside a model whether texts are in it, and both apply the test to copyrighted books)
