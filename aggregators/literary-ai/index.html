The Literary History of Artificial Intelligence

https://literaryai.library.columbia.edu/about/

historical lineageField history and indexes of electronic, generative and computational literaturehistorical
The Literary History of Artificial Intelligence, also called Literary AI, is an online exhibit of Columbia University's Rare Book & Manuscript Library on the shared history of literature and computation, told through Columbia's holdings from circa 1890 to 1970.

What it is

It is a collaboration between the Columbia English Department, the Rare Book & Manuscript Library and Columbia's Digital Scholarship department, laid out in eight cases, its site copyright 2022 by Columbia University Libraries under a CC-BY 4.0 International License. No founding date is stated, and Wikipedia has no article under its title.

Two stories

The first story, of production, follows writing manuals and plot generators such as Wycliffe Hill's Plot Genies and William Wallace Cook's Plotto into mid-twentieth-century computer experiments, from Christopher Strachey's love letter program of the 1940s to Margaret Masterman's work on automatic translation.

The second story, of analysis, runs from Harriot Fansler's Types of Prose Narratives and Georges Polti's Les trente-six situations dramatiques (1895) to the techniques for organizing story elements that, in the exhibit's account, forecast later information storage.

What its record can settle

  • which manuals, devices, patents and early programs for producing and analysing literature Columbia's library holds, circa 1890–1970
  • how the exhibit relates early writing techniques to later natural language processing, in its own account

What it cannot settle

  • whether any particular text entered a machine-learning model
  • anything after circa 1970, beyond the about page's claims of influence on contemporary tools
  • how generated text is attributed in the items shown (the cases are unread)

Kept apart

  • NOT evidence that any text entered a model: it is a library exhibition of circa 1890–1970 material
  • NOT a corpus or dataset
  • NOT a record of writing addressed to a machine reader: its items treat the machine as method or writer
