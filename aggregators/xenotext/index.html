The Xenotext

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Xenotext

historical lineageWriting addressed to a receiving apparatusReception becoming compositionactive
The archive's reading. #186's xeno-address lineage, and under #1543's substrate-neutral genus TLL* a cross-substrate comparative case: a poem whose transmission design addresses a receiving carrier that is to read it and emit a second poem, with fluorescence designed as the readback signal. Its design and its implementation are separate records, the latter known here only from Wikipedia (a half-size protein in 2011, full expression in E. coli reported in 2012, incomplete expression in the target extremophile in 2015), and none of it is AI training.

Unit of recordthe work: two fourteen-line poems, 'Orpheus' and 'Eurydice', mutual ciphers of each other under the Xenocode, with the DNA plasmid that carries the first and the protein that is to carry the second
What it recordsa poem encoded as a strand of DNA for implantation into the bacterium Deinococcus radiodurans, designed so that the bacterium reads the strand and produces a protein that is also an intelligible poem; and the implementation record, as Wikipedia reports it, from 2011 onward
In the layernot applicable: the receiving carrier is a bacterium (E. coli in the reported tests, D. radiodurans as the target); no machine-learning training is involved
Under whose nameas Wikipedia reports Bök's account, 'Orpheus' is 'written by me' and 'Eurydice' is 'written by the microbe'; Bök hopes 'to enlist the microorganism as a co-author of the poem'; the second poem is fixed by Bök's cipher, which he worked out with a Perl program over four years
Found and read backdesigned: the protein is tagged with the red fluorescent protein mcherry, so red fluorescence signals that the protein was made; reported: in April 2011 an electrophoresis showed the protein at half the expected size despite fluorescing; the second poem is read back by translating the amino acid sequence through Bök's substitution cipher
What the apparatus makes from itdesigned: from the DNA-poem the carrier makes a protein that encodes a second legible poem; reported by Wikipedia: half-size protein in E. coli (April 2011, Bök's 'first microbial critic'), full expression in E. coli reported by DNA 2.0 on 3 October 2012, and in D. radiodurans a protein 'not fully expressed' (Bök, 2015 interview)
Methoda substitution cipher (the Xenocode) that pairs letters as DNA and RNA codons pair, two poems composed as mutual ciphers, protein folding simulated with Rosetta, the DNA-poem synthesized as a plasmid and implanted in bacteria; published in books (The Xenotext: Book I, 2015; Book II, 2025)
Coveragegeography: laboratories at the University of Calgary and the University of Wyoming, and DNA 2.0, as Wikipedia reports; period: conceived as early as 2002; poems finished spring 2011; implementation reported 2011–2015; Book II published 3 June 2025

What its record can settle

  • how the work is designed: a poem that a biological carrier is to read and answer with a second poem, under a fixed cipher
  • what Wikipedia reports of the implementation from 2011 to 2015
  • how Bök assigns authorship between himself and the microbe, as Wikipedia quotes him

What it cannot settle

  • whether the protein-poem is now stably expressed in D. radiodurans (the saved record stops at the 2015 interview)
  • what Book II documents of the experiment (unread)
  • Bök's own 2011 account, which the saved sources do not hold (HTTP 403)
  • anything about machine-learning models or training data

Kept apart

  • NOT AI training: the receiving carrier is a bacterium
  • NOT the books: The Xenotext: Book I and Book II are publications of the project, linked objects of this work
  • NOT independently verified here: the implementation record rests on Wikipedia, a third party; Bök's own 2011 account is unread (HTTP 403)
  • NOT a completed implementation in its target organism as of the saved record: the 2015 interview reports the protein 'not fully expressed'
  • NOT the 2011 account as a record of outcome: Wikipedia places that article at the moment of 'apparent success', before the April 2011 electrophoresis result
