The training layer: a meta-aggregator / The Pile

The Pile

https://pile.eleuther.ai/

infrastructureThe corporahistorical
The Pile is an open-source dataset of English text that EleutherAI assembled to train large language models, twenty-two smaller datasets combined into one, released on 31 December 2020.

What it is

Its paper, by Leo Gao, Stella Biderman and ten others, presents it as a training dataset and as a broad-coverage benchmark. Its size is given three ways: 800GB in the paper's title, 825 GiB on its home page and 886.03 GB in Wikipedia's table of components. Its components include Pile-CC, its own extraction from Common Crawl, Books3, a dataset of books derived from a copy of the contents of the Bibliotik private tracker, and PG-19, Project Gutenberg books from before 1919.

What a model trained on it makes

It is also a benchmark, Pile BPB, bits per UTF-8 encoded byte. EleutherAI described GPT-Neo, released early in 2021, as the first LLM trained on data its creators had published, and GPT-J was trained on it as well. As of 2024 it was, with Common Crawl, one of the two main training datasets used to train AI models.

The census record

The archive's reading. The archive reads the Pile as the training layer named and itemized: a work enters by belonging to a component (Books3, PG-19, Pile-CC), and what the receiving apparatus makes from it is a trained model, scored on the Pile's own benchmark. Its own pages offer no per-work search; the corpus has since been inspected from outside (Data Portraits documents it), and Wikipedia's record of the Books3 removal and the authors' suit is the field's clearest case of works found in the layer under their authors' names after the fact.

What its record can settle

  • which 22 component datasets make up the Pile and their sizes
  • how a model scores on Pile BPB
  • how the Pile was meant to improve on Raw CC and CC-100 for training

What it cannot settle

  • whether a given work is in the Pile (no per-work search in its own pages)
  • under what author name a work sits in a component
  • where the Pile is now hosted (own page names the Eye; Wikipedia says it was taken down from its original site by 2024)

Kept apart

  • NOT Common Pile v0.1, its 2025 successor of licensed works
  • NOT searchable by work in its own pages
  • NOT the same size in every source: 800GB, 825 GiB and 886.03 GB are all given
  • NOT Common Crawl: Pile-CC is one of its 22 components, and the saved sources do not state its derivation
