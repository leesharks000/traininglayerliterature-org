The training layer: a meta-aggregator / The Readers Project

The Readers Project

https://thereadersproject.org/

historical lineageReception becoming compositionWriting addressed to a receiving apparatushistorical
The archive's reading. The archive reads the readers as an authored receiving apparatus whose reading emits writing, the σ : ⟨x, M₀⟩ → ⟨x, M₁, y⟩ of #1543 built by hand before the large models: the project states that its readers, 'composed by the words within which they move', also 'write', and How It Is in Common Tongues carries Beckett's text through a search engine's index and back as recomposition with each phrase sourced to a URL, a date and a count, a pre-LLM case of reading-to-composition with phrase-level provenance kept, near the search-layer lineage #186 assigns to Flarf. It is electronic literature with an index and a search engine as its apparatus; the saved sources make no claim about model training.

What it recordssoftware readers (Simple, Perigram, Spawning, Mesostic, Grammatical) released onto found texts, reconfigured appropriated sources and the collaborators' own compositions; the texts those readers generate; How It Is in Common Tongues, a recomposition of Samuel Beckett's How It Is from phrases found by a search engine
In the layerthe readers read the texts they are released onto and draw word and phrase frequencies 'culled from the largest corpus of human language that has ever existed', made visible by web indexing; How It Is in Common Tongues quotes phrases from 'a portion of the commons of language that happens to have been indexed'; the saved sources name no model training
Under whose namethe readers' outputs are published as the project's work under Cayley and Howe; in How It Is in Common Tongues each found phrase is cited by URL, date and occurrence count, and the saved page states no author names for the phrases, which it says were 'composed by writers or writing machines other than Beckett'
Found and read backthe project states it displays 'the primary source of a text generator's inputs' within that source's typographic environment, so the reading path can be followed on the source text; How It Is in Common Tongues gives for each phrase the shortest URL the readers retrieved, the date found and the total occurrences on that date, in hexadecimal notes
What the apparatus makes from itnew text generated in the course of reading: perigram readers diverge and generate 'an alternative text', a mesostic reader capitalizes letters in place to spell out a phrase, a grammatical reader jumps ahead 'leaving strange lacuna in the text while still preserving aspects of its style'; How It Is in Common Tongues recomposes Beckett's novel in sequence from the longest common phrases found elsewhere
Methodcoded reading behaviors derived from 'a necessarily partial, aesthetically implicated analysis of human reading', moving through a text's typographic neighborhood and testing 3-gram completions against natural-language frequency data; How It Is in Common Tongues by searching Beckett's text through a search engine phrase by phrase

What its record can settle

  • how each reader type moves through a text and what text it generates
  • from which URL, on which date and at what frequency each phrase of How It Is in Common Tongues was found
  • which critics and venues discussed the project, as the project lists them

What it cannot settle

  • whether any of the readers' outputs or source texts entered a training corpus or a model
  • who wrote each phrase recomposed in How It Is in Common Tongues (the notes give URLs, with no author names on the saved page)
  • whether the readers or Readers LIVE still run (not stated on the saved pages)

Kept apart

  • NOT an LLM or model-training experiment: the readers are coded reading behaviors on texts and an index
  • NOT the ELC3 entry: the Electronic Literature Collection, Volume 3 holds the collection's record of this project, which is the same project
  • NOT How It Is in Common Tongues alone: that artists' book is one work of the project, recorded inside this row
  • NOT a corpus of attributed phrases: How It Is in Common Tongues cites each phrase by URL, with no author names shown on the saved page

Relations

  • neighbour: Electronic Literature Collection (the Electronic Literature Collection, Volume 3 carries the collection's record of this project (work=the-readers-project); the project's pages link it as 'ELC3')
  • neighbour: ELMCIP Electronic Literature Knowledge Base (the research page lists an ELMCIP record of the project (2013) and ELMCIP Knowledge Base entries for The Readers Project, Common Tongues, How It Is in Common Tongues and The Image)
  • neighbour: ReRites (the research page quotes David Jhave Johnston's Aesthetic Animism on the project; John Cayley writes an essay in the ReRites response volume (Anteism pages))
  • neighbour: Output: An Anthology of Computer-Generated Text, 1953–2023 (Nick Montfort, co-editor of Output, noted How It Is in Common Tongues as a Radical Book of 2012)
