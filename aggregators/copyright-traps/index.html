Copyright Traps for Large Language Models

https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/meeus24a.html

empirical comparatorInspectionhistorical
The archive's reading. A controlled experiment on marking a work so that its passage into training can be read back later, and it records the limits of that readback as well as its success: a medium-length trap repeated 100 times stayed undetectable, a long heavily repeated one reached AUC=0.75. It is a detection method; #186's address criterion, writing that anticipates machine reading as part of its reception, lies outside what the paper studies, and the paper gives no ground to describe training-layer writing as poisoning.

What its record can settle

  • whether inserted trap sequences of a given length and repetition can be detected afterward in a 1.3B model trained on them
  • whether existing document-level inference methods detect content in a model that does not naturally memorize
  • how repetition and perplexity bear on memorization, in a controlled setup

What it cannot settle

  • whether a given published work is in a deployed model's training data (the experiment trains its own model)
  • under whose name a work sits in the training data
  • what a model writes from the content it was trained on
  • results for models larger than the one trained (the abstract reports a 1.3B model)

Kept apart

  • NOT a literary genre: a detection method tested in a controlled experiment
  • NOT a warrant to call training-layer writing poisoning: the traps are fictitious entries for detecting use
  • NOT a guarantee of detection: medium-length traps repeated 100 times were undetectable
  • NOT a test of a deployed model: the experiment trains its own 1.3B LLM
  • NOT a record of attribution: the trap marks content, and the abstract records nothing of names

Relations

  • neighbour: Min-K% Prob (both address inference from black-box access to a model whether a piece of content was seen in training; the traps abstract names existing 'SOTA methods' without naming Min-K% Prob)
