Project Gutenberg

https://www.gutenberg.org/

The corporaactive
The archive's reading. The archive reads Project Gutenberg as the public domain shelf that #186 notes is 'as a matter of fact, in the corpora' (per Wikipedia, as the Pile's Gutenberg (PG-19) component). Its own terms address two readers apart: the website 'is intended for human users only', and the machine is sent to mirrors and a machine-readable catalog.
Kindonline library of free eBooks
Operatorthe Project Gutenberg Literary Archive Foundation, which operates Project Gutenberg and accepts donations; per Wikipedia, a 501(c)(3) chartered in Mississippi in 2000, with Eric Hellman acting CEO after Gregory Newby died in 2025
Evidencedigital library
Unit of recordan eBook: a digitized, proofread text with its own permanent landing page (e.g. www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11), released in plain text and other formats
What it records'the world's literature' digitized and proofread by volunteers; per Wikipedia, mostly full texts of books or individual stories in the public domain in the United States, primarily works of literature from the Western cultural tradition, most in English
In the layerpublic domain eBooks; per Wikipedia, the Pile carries a 'Gutenberg (PG-19)' component of 11.68 GB; Project Gutenberg's own saved pages say nothing of training
Under whose namenot recorded for authors in the saved own pages; Wikipedia records that 'Project Gutenberg' is a trademark that cannot be used in commercial or modified redistributions, that public domain texts may be resold once all references to Project Gutenberg are removed, and that books have been sold stripped of attribution to the project
Found and read backeach eBook has a permanent landing page; site search; a Gutenberg index file listing all books and a machine-readable catalog database (Offline Catalogs and Feeds); the website itself 'is intended for human users only', and bulk downloads go through mirrors
Methodvolunteer digitization and proofreading; per Wikipedia, no selection policy (volunteers work on what interests them), copyright clearance recorded before a text is added, and close affiliation with Distributed Proofreaders
Coveragegeography: target audience United States persons; works not protected by copyright in the United States; most releases in English, many in other languages; period: eBooks since 1971
Countvalue: more than 75,000 free eBooks (own about page); nearly 78,000 items (Wikipedia, as of 22 March 2026); as_of: about page as read 2026-11-01; Wikipedia figure 2026-03-22
Holdingsmore than 75,000 free eBooks; releases in plain text and in HTML, EPUB and MOBI (Wikipedia); per Wikipedia, novels, poetry, short stories and drama, with cookbooks, reference works and issues of periodicals
Stated limits'Project Gutenberg’s target audience is United States persons over 13 years of age.'; 'Project Gutenberg offers no warranty or assurances about copyright status or freedom to access or use its materials outside of the United States.'; 'This website is intended for human users only.'; more than ~100 books per day are to be downloaded from mirrors; per Wikipedia, the Text Encoding Initiative criticized it in 1994 for not documenting which version of a text was digitized
Data accessfree eBooks on gutenberg.org with permanent landing pages; mirrors for bulk download; the Gutenberg index file; Offline Catalogs and Feeds (a machine-readable database); an OPDS feed under stated rules for applications
Licencethe Project Gutenberg License (unread); 'All Project Gutenberg eBooks are completely free and always will be'; per Wikipedia, most books are public domain under US law and a few copyrighted texts are distributed with permission
Lifecyclestate: active; detail: operating since 1971; per Wikipedia, about 30 new eBooks a week (May 2025)
Version and timecount_wikipedia: 2026-03-22; count_own: more than 75,000 (about page); read: 2026-11-01
Founded1971 (Michael Hart)

What its record can settle

  • whether a public domain work is held as a Project Gutenberg eBook, and at which landing page
  • in which formats a release is available
  • on what terms bulk and automated access is allowed

What it cannot settle

  • which edition a text was digitized from (per Wikipedia, not always documented)
  • whether a given eBook was carried into a given training set (the Pile's PG-19 component is known here from Wikipedia)
  • how its texts are attributed once redistributed elsewhere

Kept apart

  • NOT a scholarly edition: per Wikipedia, the version digitized is not always documented
  • NOT open to automated access on its main site: its terms send bulk and automated downloads to mirrors
  • NOT a training dataset: the PG-19 component is the Pile's, known here from Wikipedia
  • NOT any of its sister projects (Project Gutenberg Australia, Projekt Gutenberg-DE and others), which Wikipedia describes as independent organizations

Relations

  • used_by: The Pile (Wikipedia's table of the Pile's sub-datasets lists 'Gutenberg (PG-19)', 11.68 GB)
  • neighbour: Internet Archive (Wikipedia lists the Internet Archive as an affiliate: previous long-time backup distribution site and previous main host site)
