Data Portraits

https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03919

infrastructureInspectionThe corporahistorical
The archive's reading. A readback instrument held on the corpus side: a portrait records the dataset so that a work can be tested for membership, which settles #186's 'is the work in the layer' for a recorded corpus. Dataset membership is not proof that a particular model used a passage, and the name and what a model made from the work (#1543's question) are outside what the saved abstract records.

What its record can settle

  • whether a given example is in a recorded dataset (The Pile, The Stack)
  • questions of test set leakage and model plagiarism against a recorded dataset, as the paper states

What it cannot settle

  • whether a particular model's output depends causally on a recorded passage
  • under whose name a recorded example sits in the dataset
  • anything about datasets that have no portrait

Kept apart

  • NOT a corpus: it records corpora built by others
  • NOT proof of a model's causal use of a passage: it records membership of a dataset
  • NOT a model-side test: it queries the recorded data and needs a portrait of it
  • NOT a record of authorship: the saved abstract says nothing of attribution metadata
  • NOT the live interface: the paper is the object read; the interface is a linked object, unread

Relations

  • uses: The Pile (the paper documents The Pile with its tools ('a popular language modeling corpus (The Pile)'))
  • neighbour: OLMoTrace (both check text against training data from outside the model's weights: Data Portraits by membership queries on a recorded corpus, OLMoTrace by verbatim span matching against the full corpus)
