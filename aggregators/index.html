The training layer: a meta-aggregator

A census of the corpora that carry writing into machine training, the tools that test whether a work is in a training set or trace an output back to training text, the readers, models and practices that make new writing from what they read, the literature composed for a machine or other carrier to read, and the field's history and indexes. Each resource is read from its own pages, and each states what it is to this census: a direct case, a historical lineage, an empirical comparator, infrastructure, a theoretical interlocutor or a peripheral comparator. Every field is quoted from a saved copy of the resource's own pages, with the date read and a hash of the copy. Compiled 2026-11-01; editor Lee Sharks.

The census asks of each resource: How is literature addressed to a receiving apparatus, what does that apparatus make from it, and how can transmission, transformation and provenance be inspected across later reception?

The question joins two deposits: Who Is Writing for the Machines? Training-Layer Literature: A Field Survey (AXN:032C, #186), the field survey of writing addressed to the machine reader, and Generative Uptake: Training-Layer Literature Extended from Machine Reading to Machine Composition (EA-TLL-COMPOSITION-01) (AXN:0642, #1543), which extends the genre from machine reading to machine composition. A work meets three steps in the resources below: its address to a receiving apparatus, what that apparatus makes from it, and whether its transmission, transformation and provenance can be inspected afterwards. Corpus membership, a matched span of output and causal influence are recorded as different things. Revised 2026-11-01 on the operator's ruling, adopting an outside selection review. The full entity pages wait for each resource's /non article.

Its sibling census, on how authorship is made, named and attributed, is the apparatus meta-aggregator at leesharks.com; the family's other censuses are Labor Vector (labor and AI) and Mary Lee Labor (left politics and labor).

OLMoTrace

a feature in the Ai2 Playground that traces language model outputs back to their full training data in real time; its paper calls it a system, released as open source · Ai2 (The Allen Institute for Artificial Intelligence), as a feature in the Ai2 Playground; the paper's authors are Jiacheng Liu and 30 others
infrastructureInspectionReception becoming compositionactive
What the apparatus makes from it: the model's response, of which only long, unique, self-contained spans with a verbatim match are highlighted (K = 0.05 times the response's token count); Ai2 shows it tracing 'creative' expressions, arithmetic and a false knowledge-cutoff claim to training documents, in its own hedged terms ('may have learned', 'could have easily learned it through memorization')

Data Portraits

The field it maps

Works, papers, practices, tools and standards read for this census and held outside it, because none of them gathers and holds the works or records of others.
