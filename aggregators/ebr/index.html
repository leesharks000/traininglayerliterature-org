The archive's reading. The journal meets the question in specific essays: Deena Larsen's 'Electronic Literature and Wikipedia: A Call to Action' holds that 'If electronic literature does not exist in Wikipedia, it does not exist for AI or search engines either', #186's persistence criterion stated from within electronic literature; its machine-writing thread (Stuart Moulthrop on the poetics of large language models, Brian Lennon's 'The Prompt as Practice', Davin Heckman's 'AI: A Matter of Significance') is criticism of machine composition, the wing #1543 names. Many of its threads since 1996 do not touch a machine reader.

What its record can settle

  • what critical writing ebr has published in a thread, by whom and when
  • who edits each thread and since when
  • what the electronic literature field is writing about AI and LLMs (the machine-writing thread)

What it cannot settle

  • whether any ebr text is in a training corpus or a model
  • how many publications it holds (not stated)
  • the creative works it discusses, held elsewhere

Kept apart

  • NOT a review of e-books: a journal of critical writing on literature, theory, criticism and the arts
  • NOT an archive of electronic literature works: it publishes criticism
  • NOT a training corpus
  • NOT one undifferentiated account of machine readers: its bearing on this census is in specific essays
